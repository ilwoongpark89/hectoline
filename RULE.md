# RULE — hectoline · version 1.1 · schema 3, see §2

This file is the method. The gate program `unit.py` in this folder reads it. No copy exists. Other folders link to it.

## 0. Rooms
- A room is a folder with UNITS.md, GOAL.md, PLAN.md, PRODUCTS.md, RESEARCH.md and OWNER.md or a link to the hub OWNER.md. It has one G0 and at most one open unit. Units open only inside a room. Its GOAL states its purpose, final outcome, acceptance checks, and parent responsibility.
- Letters, six and no other. A is an owner statement in OWNER.md. G is a goal in GOAL.md. S is a step in PLAN.md. R is a research claim in RESEARCH.md. U is a unit in UNITS.md. P is a product in PRODUCTS.md. Numbers are flat, hierarchy sits in a parent column.
- The chain is one way, A → G → S → U → P. A statement makes or changes a goal. A goal is reached through steps. A step makes or changes one product through units. R feeds G and S. A row without a parent in the layer above cannot exist. A planning unit and an audit unit hang on their A row, with no S between.
- Sizes, ten by ten by ten by ten, convention until R rows replace them. A room holds at most 10 goal leaves, 100 steps, 1000 units and 10 parent products. A goal leaf has 2 to 10 steps. A step has 1 to 10 units. A unit touches one product. A structural move touches the product it leaves and the product it enters. It also touches the import and call lines of their callers, with no behavior change.
- A ledger of the six letters removes no row or block and is exempt from the 500-line leaf cap. The report shows the last ten blocks of UNITS.md in full.
- Products form a tree by parent, convention until R rows replace the numbers. A leaf P holds at most 500 lines, a code product 500 accepted lines, §1. A parent P is a folder with at most ten children and no lines of its own. A product at its cap takes a splitting step before any other step. The split follows one responsibility boundary, named in its cause key. The boundaries are rule decisions, transitions with their writes, measures and views. The review refuses a split by line count alone.
- A hub room is the repo root of one project. Owner statements live there. It holds the G tree and the top P rows, each naming the leaf that owns it. A code hub holds no product code, and its units are planning and audit units. A data hub holds its documents, its index and one measure tool, and its units may import them.
- A leaf room is a subfolder of the hub for one necessary product boundary. Read the complete parent context before defining it. Record applied constraints and source references in existing research and product records. Its GOAL names the parent outcome and returned product. The parent index names that responsibility and product. Existing sibling leaves retain their declared routes during coordinated migration. It holds code and its own UNITS, GOAL, PLAN, PRODUCTS, RESEARCH. Its G0 serves one declared parent goal. Its plan keys cite hub A numbers as «A#### @hub». A room cites a hub row only in the units that make it. Later units cite their own A rows.
- Leaves declare cross-leaf uses in PRODUCTS.md. An import without a declaration is denied. Each parallel terminal works in its own git worktree, one leaf per worktree.
- The planning unit measures three numbers first. Outcomes in the statement. Size of the pile in lines and import clusters. Terminals. One outcome, at most 50 000 lines, at most 300 expected units and one terminal make one room. Two outcomes, two import clusters or two terminals make a hub with one leaf each. The 1000 audit decides a split or a rebuild. The numbers are convention until R rows replace them.
- The folder that holds this rule and its tools is the rule room. Every other room links to it.
- A room folder under the method carries a number prefix. The rule room is 00, the first project hub 01, its first leaf 01_01. Before creation, use the project map to find the existing owner, evidence, and canonical product. Reuse them when their responsibility matches. A blocked edit does not justify a new folder. A new child belongs inside its parent and carries that parent's number. Its number is not proof of reading or gate coverage. A room has a PROMPT.md, the first message of its terminal and nothing more. Flows live in GOAL.md and PLAN.md. The terminal decides. The owner's share is money, signature, external sending, deploy approval, data deletion and judgment about people. No question goes to the owner outside it and the questions of §3 and §4.

## 1. Unit
- One unit is one logical change of at most 100 added or modified lines. The ledger block counts. Moved lines are listed, not counted. A code line is one statement of at most 10 words. Words are the name, number and string tokens of tokenize, operators uncounted. `lines.py` counts them. An accepted line passes `lines.py`. The caps of 100 and 500 count accepted lines.
- Five tests. One sentence without «and». Revertable alone. One type. Hides one design decision. Verified alone by a check that fails before the unit and passes after it.
- GOAL.md is the goal page of the common rule §1. Its table holds the goal checks, each with a baseline. A leaf whose number no tool of the room computes is unmeasured. Under it only its measure step opens.
- Types. structural: no behavior change, documents and moves included. behavioral: one behavior enters or changes. redesign: replaces a closed or failed unit. Its deleted key of §2 names the lines it removes, or the failed block it replaces. audit: a periodic check, deletions only.
- The room is the folder under this rule. A pile is any line in the room that no block names. Lines in the legacy folder of §6 are not piles. Lines that `unit.py` writes, the log `unit.log`, are not piles. `unit.py` maps each line to its unit through `git blame`. The close or fail commit message starts with the unit number. Figures, tables and pages come from a script inside a unit. A hand-made binary file is a pile.

## 2. Ledger
- `UNITS.md` in the room root. Blocks are appended, never removed or reordered. `unit.py open` appends. Only open and waiting blocks change. A later block names what it replaces in its deleted key.
- Header `## U#### · YYYY-MM-DD · type · status`, then the keys of the table, one per line, in its order. A planned block holds five, sentence, plan, files, base, lines. open adds the rest. Status: planned, open, waiting, closed, failed. Closed and failed are final. One open block at a time. waiting means the author waits for a review or a dispatch.
- A `## schema N` line marks a schema change. Blocks after it follow schema N. Blocks before it keep their schema. Blocks before the first marker are schema 1.

| key | meaning | must hold |
|---|---|---|
| sentence | the one change | no «and» |
| plan | the A row of §3 it serves and the S step it belongs to | an A number and an S number. «S none» only for a planning unit, an audit unit, or while PLAN.md does not exist. A planning unit of §3 writes the A numbers it adds. The ledger unit, the rule unit and the goal unit write «bootstrap» |
| delete? | could deletion alone solve it | yes turns the unit into a deletion |
| modify? | could an existing block change instead | yes turns the unit into that change |
| duplicate | the search command run and its hits | a command and its count at HEAD, written by the script that ran it |
| cause | the layer of inputs → rules → state → result → view the change enters, and why there | names the layer |
| split? | could it enter existing places | yes splits the unit |
| tidy-first? | must structure change first | yes opens a structural unit first |
| check | shell commands, separated by « · », and no clause contains that separator | each exits non-zero before, 0 after. A clause tests the content of its own lines or rows, not a whole-file count or state. A clause opens by testing that the moved or added definition exists. A planning clause greps no planned status. No clause asserts its own block's status |
| files | path:range | budget «path:≤N» while open, actual at close. A range in a closed block is the range at close |
| base | what the unit stands on | planned: the unit it waits for. open: that unit and its commit. «none» only for the ledger unit |
| lines | +added ~modified −deleted moved | budget «≤N» while open, actual at close, added plus modified ≤ 100 |
| added | what entered | filled at close |
| rejected | what was considered and left out, with reason | never empty at close |
| split | what was broken apart, into where | «none» or names |
| deleted | what left, from where | «none» or names |
| result | the check output | filled at close. Every count a key reports, a budget aside, is the output of the command the key names, by its script |
| review | pass line: model, session id, log path | filled before close, not the author session |
| approval | the owner line, verbatim | the quote of the A row in plan. A protected path of §4 or a §6 import needs an owner line that names the unit. GOAL.md may demand that line for every unit. Filled at open for a protected path |

- `PLAN.md` holds one S row per step: S number, parent G, product P, interface, units, status. The interface is two short lines, what the product exposes and what it hides. It stands before the first unit of the step opens. Status: todo, open, done. An S row names in its text the leaf number it moves and the move it expects. A pilot under G0 names the measurable movement of its descendant leaves. A measure compatibility step can name a fixture baseline while preserving the production count. Close checks each new planned step with the same eligibility rules as open. A step is done when every unit under it is final, the last one closed, and its number moved. Else its last unit names the reason and the next unit is a redesign. The work count = done steps ÷ all steps.
- A check proves an outcome. A check that only proves that text exists is not a check. A selftest clause binds the S mark of its case, not the total. A clause on an S row accepts open or done. A number outside the six letters of §0 is a fail-grade finding, and so is a row without a parent.
- The nine verdict keys are sentence to check. planned fills sentence, plan, base, files, lines. open fills the nine, and base, files, lines when the block is new. closed fills the rest with actuals. split may hold «none» at open. failed fills result with the reason and reverts the lines. A redesign fills deleted at open.

## 3. Owner statements
- `OWNER.md` holds one A row per owner statement: A number, date, kind, verbatim quote, refined goal, parent A, status.
- Kind: order, question, idea, answer, defect. A defect is an owner statement that names a fault in a delivered product. Its row stays active until a unit changes the structure that let the fault through. Then its status turns done with that unit. Status: active, done, superseded by A, dropped by A, conflict A.
- A new statement that collides with an active A gets status conflict. No unit under either opens. One question goes to the owner: «A#### is no longer considered?». The answer is a new A row.
- Every owner instruction starts with one planning unit. It works in four steps. The A rows. The goals the statement changes. The S steps under the changed goals, each with its product and interface. Then at most three planned blocks cut from the first steps. It closes before any work unit opens. The bootstrap units need no planning unit. The first step under G0 is the pilot, one instance of the final product end to end. Its first planning unit adds the pilot row. Before it closes only the measures and the pilot open.
- A planning unit and an audit unit carry no step. Their plan key says «S none» with the reason.
- The planning unit also runs when the planned queue is empty or a leaf flips. Candidates are todo steps under unmet leaves. Priority runs in five ranks. A redesign forced by a fail. An audit due. An A in conflict. The measure of an unmeasured leaf. The leaf farthest from its target by its own number. An explicit owner order overrides the last two. The chosen candidates stand in added, the rest in rejected with reasons.

## 4. Gates
- The block exists before the first edit of any file it names. `unit.py open` appends the block from four texts, sentence, plan, files and lines, or turns its planned block open. That edit is the block. A planning block enters over an empty queue or through the queue repair gate. Queue repair requires no open or waiting unit and no overdue audit. At least one planned block must be base-ready. Each base-ready planned block must fail step eligibility. The repair owns OWNER.md, PLAN.md and its own UNITS.md block only. The open log records its admission and baseline commit. Edit and close retain this restriction even if its plan or files key changes. Close preserves all earlier unit blocks and rejects remaining eligibility errors. Product changes are forbidden. A pile blocks commit and turn end. A pile has one exit: deletion in the same turn. Code worth keeping goes to the legacy folder first and enters through §6. `base.py map` prints the room map from the ledgers at each call and never stores it. The map is the chain A → G → S → U → P as one tree of ids. Each id shows its one-line head and its citers. It holds no verdict, and its first line reads «the ledgers win over this map». `unit.py open` prints the unit pack of `base.py pack`. It holds the leaf, the step, the A quotes and the R rows that name the step. The pack also gives each file of the files key with its last three units and their fail notes. The author reads the pack and greps the ledgers, never the ledgers whole.
- `unit.py open` refuses a unit whose base is not final. It refuses any unit while another is open.
- A line has a chain of owners: `git blame` gives the current one, its block's deleted key the earlier ones. `unit.py check` refuses, before close, a unit that modifies a line with two earlier owners, unless the type is redesign. A redesign's deleted key reaches its non-ledger files only. A ledger row it touched keeps one owner.
- The review runs while the unit stays open. A separate reviewer reads the room and writes its log outside the room. The review key retains every round. One review record serves closure, waiting locks, repair admission, measures, and views. Labels are case-insensitive and accept `r1 pass` or `pass r1`. The highest numbered round supplies the verdict. Quoted labels are evidence text only. Same-round contradictions and malformed labels are unknown. A standalone verdict without rounds retains its legacy meaning. Waiting permits product edits only after a nonterminal failure. The shared ledger remains editable by its unit. Waiting permits closure after a pass or terminal failure. A second unit opens beside a waiting unit only when their product paths do not overlap. Stop passes when the pile is empty.
- Every review asks one more question: does the change stay behind the interface of its step?
- A review reads the diff, the nine verdict keys and the RULE sections the block names. It runs check first and searches nothing outside the room except the owner transcript for quotes. Its log holds at most 20 lines. Reuse one or two independent reviewer sessions and keep their context. Record the actual model and session. An unavailable reviewer can be replaced by another independent available session. Never infer reviewer identity from a commit trailer.
- An audit finding carries its command and its output, or it is not a finding. Style and taste are not findings. An audit script reruns the checks of non-audit units only, on a copy of the room.
- A structural change to the method enters only as an A row or an audit finding. Its cause key lists every rule line, ledger shape and closed check it touches. A planning unit cuts it into units of at most five decisions. A decision is one line of this file or one function of a tool. RULE first, then the files, then the tool.
- A full inspection runs on an owner order or at the 100 audit. The order is fixed. Every A. The products as they are. Each goal measure. The steps and units. Then the output. The output is one planning unit with goals corrected, steps revised, the next units planned.
- `unit.py close` requires a passing check, review, approval, and rejected key for successful closure. Unknown reviews and unresolved failures cannot authorize success. A terminal failure instead follows the rollback rule below. The review key names the review log file in dispatch as proof of outside review. The `open` log line records the session id from the environment. The log is `unit.log` in the room root. `unit.py close` prints the impact list. It names every A, G, S, R and P id whose row the diff changes. Each id comes with the tracked files that cite it. The review brief carries the list, and the review reads each citer. The selftest hides a citer and expects the list to name it. `unit.py close` refuses a diff whose added code lines fail `lines.py diff`.
- The third failed verification ends the unit. Count distinct failed review rounds plus persistent failed close-verification events. One close attempt adds one event for a failed result check or passing baseline check. Expected red probes and edit refusals do not count. A later pass cannot cancel three failures. `unit.py close` sets status failed, records the reason, reverts its product changes, and commits under the unit number. Explicit withdrawal starts the review key with `fail, withdrawn` and gives its reason. It uses the same rollback before the limit. The next unit on those lines is a redesign.
- The same goal reopens at once as a redesign: new number, base the failed unit, deleted names the failed block. Its first verdict is split?. A failed redesign stops the goal. Its A row turns conflict. One question goes to the owner: continue, split, hold or drop. No third redesign. A skip needs an owner line and a dropped A row. A hold keeps the row in conflict. The planned blocks of a failed planning unit stay in UNITS.md, its rows go back with its other paths. Its redesign takes one of them as its own block and re-points the rest.
- Review convergence: the second and later reviews receive the earlier findings, verify each fix first, then add only fail-grade findings. Fail grade is one of nine, the seven here and the two of §2. A rule or field that cannot work. A false field. A false claim about another file. A quote not verbatim. A status outside the set. A check that passes at the base. A step that names no leaf move. Weak lines never fail a unit.
- Protected paths: RULE.md, unit.py, ledger.py, gate.py, selftest.py, `hooks/`, `.git/hooks/`, `~/.claude/settings.json`, `~/.codex/config.toml`. An edit there needs an owner line that names the unit. `unit.log` is append-only by `unit.py`, by `ledger.py`, and hooks deny any other write to it.
- Agent hooks deny `--no-verify`, `-n` on git commit, and `core.hooksPath`. A test file, under tests/ or named test_* or *_test.*, in the same unit as code outside tests is denied. A commit that reached the repo without a final unit is a pile, and the next audit lists it. A new room starts empty only after the ownership check. An existing project adopts gates in place through a reviewed baseline. The baseline records unchanged existing files, not new code. Baseline records do not certify earlier work. The constructor must validate that baseline before product edits. Every later change enters through a unit. The legacy folder is read through its frozen link, §6.
- Hooks: Claude Code PreToolUse and PostToolUse return exit 2 on a failed check. Its Stop requests pile cleanup with exit 2. Codex PreToolUse denies before execution with permissionDecision. PostToolUse returns block feedback after execution. Stop continues work for pile cleanup with a block decision. Installation does not establish trust or complete write coverage. The git pre-commit runs validate then pile and denies with exit 1. Edit hooks call unit.py check. Each check decision has one log line. The pre-commit prints its refusal.
- Audit every 10 units, every 100, every 1000, and once when the goal checks are met. Failed units count, audit units do not. After every tenth unit, `open` accepts only an audit unit, or one planning unit that plans it. That holds until an audit unit closes with status closed. From ten on, the four texts open the audit over a planned queue, and past ten over an empty one. Its base is the last final block. A failed audit is followed by another audit unit. The 10 level reruns every check of a non-audit unit and lists piles, duplicates, unused code and goals without units. It prints each leaf's number against the last audit. A step closed without a move is a finding. The 100 level remeasures the goal table, removes rules that never fired and lists the restructuring. A refusal with a passing case stays as a guard. Removal is for a sentence with no tool and no case. The 10 level prints the never-fired table from the log, the deny reasons against the refusals of check. The restructuring list gives each lesson of the last 100 units and the line or tool it folds into. It names the text each lesson supersedes. It names the ledger views to print in place of rereading, and the raw ledgers stay. Its planning unit cuts the list into units, so a lesson changes a line and never appends one. The 1000 level compares the structure with a blank design, a design written from GOAL.md alone without reading the code.

## 5. Report
- `unit.py status` prints the state of a room from its ledgers, the log and git. Ledger cells enter as they stand. No number is typed.
- Progress = leaves at target ÷ leaves. A leaf shows its number and its number at the last audit. An unmeasured leaf shows so. Done steps ÷ all steps is the work count, not progress. Each goal leaf carries one measure, a number `unit.py status` computes from the ledgers, the log or git. External adapter imports and calls run only in a confined worker. The worker receives requested goals and the ledger snapshot. Product writes and network access are denied. Only its bounded protocol file and /dev/null permit writes. Runtime is bounded and its process group is reaped. Missing confinement, invalid output, or adapter failure stays unmeasured. Caller caches cannot supply worker results.
- The number moves only when a unit closes. A typed number is a pile. Every table and report to the owner opens with «progress N %, a / b», then the bundles.
- The composition block shows hooks active, product sizes against caps, units over 100 lines, and missing generation markers. It also shows failed units, review rounds, denies, piles, and audits. A missing measure states its reason. Generation markers identify a format, not authorship or provenance.

## 6. Legacy
- The legacy folder holds the old work. It is frozen, edits there are denied, deletions allowed. A frozen source receives a link when the declared product boundary requires it. Gate adoption does not freeze an active project. Keep its canonical products with their existing owner. New code is written. The legacy may run as an instrument, its output kept outside it. It is never read into the new code, copied or edited. Its default values and readings enter as an R row or a test baseline. The new result may be compared with them. The new code must run better.
- The legacy opens for one design question at a time, a question the goal cannot answer alone. The answer is an R row with the file, the line and the command run. No inventory, no rank, no zero target.
- A piece enters only when a step needs it, in two units. First a characterization test records the old behavior as it is. Then the import unit rewrites the piece into the new structure, never a copy. A titled, dated, sourced document moves whole, unread, in one structural unit with an owner line.
- An import unit carries an owner approval line. What no step needs stays in the legacy, uncounted and unlisted. The check of a unit that adds R rows finds each quote in its source text. A legacy folder ends as an archive, never as a live folder or a plain deletion of the whole folder. It moves when four conditions hold. The room's leaves are measured, and its pilot runs end to end. No step runs the legacy as an instrument. No terminal works in the legacy. The owner names the move in one line. Then a tar file and its sha256 go to the backup. The room keeps the file list with the hash of each file. The folder and its link leave the working tree.

## 7. Research
- Research is a unit, type structural. Its sentence is the question and ends with a question mark, the mark `unit.py check` reads. Search or dispatch happens only while it is open. Hooks deny WebSearch, WebFetch and Agent outside one. Agent passes outside one for the review of a live unit when its prompt names the agent-review file of §4.
- `RESEARCH.md` holds one R row per source claim. Columns: R, date, question, source, quote or number, pages read of pages, support count, counter count, verdict, user. Each row gives its source with URL or with the legacy file and the command run. The user is an A, a G or a rule line.
- A rule or goal line that rests on research names its R. A line without R says «owner» or «convention».
- A research unit's check: R rows ≥ N, with N set at open. Each row holds URL or the legacy file, date, quote, and pages read equal to pages. A legacy source holds the command run in place of the pages. Its duplicate key starts from RESEARCH.md. A new search opens only when no R row holds the number.
- Dispatch only when the searches would not finish in ten minutes. The agent count stands in cause. Dispatch folders stay the raw record, the R row points to them.
